# Visual Film Language: Density Rhythm

Density controls breathing. Dense areas require high cognitive load. Empty areas force exhalation. We alternate extreme density with extreme emptiness.

## The Breathing Pattern

### 1. Inhale (Extreme Emptiness)
- **Layout:** The viewport is entirely warm paper color (RGB: 247, 243, 238). In the absolute center, one sentence is rendered in 4rem serif text: "The river remembers." 
- **Density:** 95% whitespace, 5% typography.
- **Effect:** Silence. The eye rests completely.

### 2. Hold Breath (High Density)
- **Layout:** A grid of six overlapping photographs (3:4, 1:1, 4:5 ratios). The images touch each other. The subjects are hands weaving, a child running, a plate of food, a wooden boat, a dog sleeping, a fire burning. A block of 14px body text, 40 characters wide, sits tightly wedged on the left edge.
- **Density:** 80% photography, 10% typography, 10% whitespace.
- **Effect:** Chaos, energy, life. The eye darts rapidly between images.

### 3. Exhale (Structured Emptiness)
- **Layout:** The viewport returns to warm paper color. A single statistic, "1924", is rendered in 12rem sans-serif text aligned to the far left. A 12px caption, "The year the first bridge was built," sits directly underneath it. The right 60% of the screen is completely blank.
- **Density:** 70% whitespace, 30% massive typography.
- **Effect:** Relief. Immediate comprehension.

### 4. Deep Breath (The Void)
- **Layout:** A 16:9 landscape image of the sky at dusk. No text. No UI. No captions.
- **Density:** 100% photography (low detail).
- **Effect:** Complete mental reset before the next information block.

## Rule of Transition
Never place a Dense block immediately after a Dense block. Never place a text-heavy block immediately after a text-heavy block. The rhythm must strictly alternate: Empty -> Dense -> Empty -> Dense.
