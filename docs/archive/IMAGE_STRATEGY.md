# Image Strategy

The current website suffers from "Image Fatigue." Images are too large, placed too frequently, and follow predictable, repetitive patterns. We are wasting vertical space and diminishing the impact of the photography.

## The Patagonia Stories Approach

### 1. The Ban on Repetitive Full-Height Images
We will no longer stack massive images on top of each other. 
*Wrong*: Full Height Image -> Full Height Image -> Full Height Image.
*Right*: Full Bleed Landscape -> Small Portrait -> Medium Square -> Collage.

### 2. Introducing the Editorial Collage
Instead of forcing the user to scroll past 6 individual photos of village life, we will combine them into a tight, art-directed collage block. This creates visual density, saves scroll space, and feels like a premium editorial layout.

### 3. The Power of the "Small Image"
Not every image needs to scream. A small, high-quality image placed next to a massive block of beautiful typography often feels more premium than a full-screen background. We will use small, asymmetrical images to create tension and elegance.

### 4. The Cinematic Pause
We will reserve full-bleed, edge-to-edge images strictly for "Cinematic Pauses." These occur every 3-4 sections. They have almost no text—just the image. They act as visual palate cleansers.

### 5. Cropping and Aspect Ratios
We will strictly control aspect ratios to create rhythm:
- **16:9 / 21:9**: For sweeping landscapes and cinematic pauses.
- **3:4 / 4:5**: For portraits and intimate details.
- **1:1**: For structured grids and structural elements.

### 6. No Decorative Imagery
Every image must earn its place. If an image is just "filler" because a section felt empty, we will remove the image and use whitespace or typography instead.

### 7. Color Grading Consistency
While we cannot change the source files, we will use CSS filters (subtly) if necessary to ensure images feel cohesive within the "Warmth & Memory" aesthetic (e.g., slightly warming up overly cool photos).
