# The Attention Map

Where does the eye go? Where does the mind linger? We do not let the visitor wander aimlessly; we direct their attention with absolute precision, like a film director framing a shot.

## Where Curiosity Begins
Curiosity is born in the **negative space**. 
When a visitor sees a tiny, beautiful portrait floating in a sea of warm paper, their eye is drawn to the portrait not because it is large, but because it is isolated. We spark curiosity by refusing to fill every inch of the screen. We give the eye nowhere else to look.

## Where Emotion Peaks
Emotion peaks in the **extreme close-up** and the **massive quote**.
- **The Visual Peak:** We pull the camera close to the weathered hands, the smiling eyes, the texture of the woven basket. 
- **The Typographic Peak:** We use massive, elegant typography for a single, devastatingly human sentence. 
When the eye hits these elements, it stops completely. The heart rate slows. The emotion lands.

## Where the User Should Pause
The pause happens in the **cinematic full-bleed landscape**.
When all text disappears and the screen is consumed by nature—mist over the river, sunrise over the fields—the visitor has no choice but to stop reading and start feeling. The absence of information forces a pause.

## Where the User Should Breathe
Breathing room is found in the **rhythm of the layout**.
After a dense, high-energy collage of village life, the next scroll must be minimal. A single short sentence. A massive expanse of soft color. This visual contrast acts as a lung expanding and contracting.

## Where the User Should Feel Nostalgia
Nostalgia lives in the **textures and the archival**.
We use faded tones, historical imagery, and the visual language of physical memory. We place a small, black-and-white or sepia-toned memory adjacent to a vibrant modern image. The contrast between then and now creates the ache of nostalgia.

## Where the User Should Smile
Smiles are triggered by **unexpected humanity**.
Not by a statistic, but by a candid photograph of a child running, an elder laughing, or a perfectly placed, lighthearted quote about village life. It is the unscripted moments, placed deliberately, that break the fourth wall.

## Where the User Should Think
Thinking happens during the **Data Climax**.
When we strip away the photography and present stark, massive numbers on a solid background, the visitor shifts from right-brain emotion to left-brain logic. They process the scale and impact of the community.

## Where the User Should Decide to Visit
The decision is made in the **Departure**.
The final view. A warm, golden-hour image. A welcoming quote. The interface fades away, leaving only the invitation. By the time they reach this point, the decision has already been planted; this final moment merely gives them permission to act on it.
