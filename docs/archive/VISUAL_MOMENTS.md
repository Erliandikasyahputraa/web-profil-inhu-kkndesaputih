# Visual Moments Strategy

An interactive documentary must have a heartbeat. If every scroll feels exactly like the last scroll, the heartbeat is flat. We must create spikes of emotion: **Visual Moments**.

A Visual Moment occurs every 2-3 standard content sections. It breaks the established layout pattern to surprise the reader.

## Types of Visual Moments

### 1. The Cinematic Full-Bleed
- **What it is**: An edge-to-edge landscape image or looping video with zero interface elements.
- **When to use it**: When transitioning between major themes (e.g., from History to Geography).
- **The Effect**: It forces the user to pause, breathe, and appreciate the scale of the environment.

### 2. The Monolithic Quote
- **What it is**: A section where the entire screen is dominated by a single, massive sentence on a warm paper background. No images.
- **When to use it**: To highlight a profound human element or philosophy of the village.
- **The Effect**: It creates intimacy and authority. The words become the art.

### 3. The Archival Collage
- **What it is**: A tightly packed, asymmetrical grid of 5-8 images overlapping slightly, resembling physical photographs dropped on a table.
- **When to use it**: To show "life in the village" (daily activities, people) without dedicating 5 vertical scrolls to individual images.
- **The Effect**: High energy, visual richness, and nostalgia.

### 4. The Data Climax
- **What it is**: A sudden shift to extreme minimalism. Just massive typography showing 3 key statistics.
- **When to use it**: To prove a point or validate the narrative (e.g., Transparency section).
- **The Effect**: Sharp, sudden clarity.

## Implementation Rule
A Visual Moment must *never* be placed immediately adjacent to another Visual Moment. They require the "connective tissue" of standard reading sections (body text, small images) to build tension before the release.
