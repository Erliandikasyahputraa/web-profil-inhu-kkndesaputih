# Visual Film Language: Eye Flow

The screen is a canvas where we dictate the exact path of the visitor's pupil. We engineer a sequence of fixations.

## Scene 1: The Quote Setup
- **First Fixation (The Anchor):** A 5rem serif word, "Listen," placed in the top left quadrant. The eye hits this instantly due to size.
- **Second Fixation (The Reading Point):** The eye drops below the anchor to read a 3-line paragraph in 16px text, max-width 45 characters.
- **Third Fixation (The Curiosity Point):** The eye scans right and hits a 200px wide, 300px high portrait image in the bottom right quadrant. 
- **Exit Point:** The visitor scrolls downward, following the implied downward gaze of the portrait.

## Scene 2: The Data Setup
- **First Fixation (The Shock):** A 15rem numeral "0" placed dead center. The contrast is extreme (dark olive text on warm paper).
- **Second Fixation (The Context):** The eye jumps to a 12px uppercase label positioned tightly inside the bowl of the "0". The label reads: "CRIME RATE".
- **Third Fixation (The Proof):** The eye moves to the bottom left, where a small, candid black-and-white photo shows an unlocked wooden door.
- **Exit Point:** The vast empty space on the right side of the screen pushes the user to scroll to find the next anchor.

## Scene 3: The Landscape Setup
- **First Fixation (The Horizon):** A full-bleed landscape image. The eye instantly tracks the horizon line cutting across the middle of the screen.
- **Second Fixation (The Discovery):** As the eye sweeps the horizon, it catches a tiny white dot in the bottom right corner.
- **Third Fixation (The Caption):** The dot is an asterisk (*). Beside it, 10px text reads: "View from Bukit Batu, 05:30 AM."
- **Exit Point:** Having resolved the visual puzzle, the eye rests on the empty sky at the bottom of the image, prompting the scroll.

## Rule of Competition
If two elements on the screen demand the First Fixation simultaneously (e.g., a massive image and a massive headline next to each other), the design is broken. We must reduce one element until the sequence is clear.
