# Typography Rewrite

Typography is the backbone of Swiss Design. Currently, the typography lacks authority. Everything feels like a medium-priority paragraph. We need extreme contrast in font sizes to guide the reader's eye and reduce cognitive load.

## The Hierarchy Rules

### 1. The Display Headline (Massive)
- **Usage**: Only for the most critical hooks.
- **Style**: Extremely large (e.g., 5rem - 8rem+). Tight leading.
- **Purpose**: To stop the scroll immediately.

### 2. The Editorial Pull Quote (Dominant)
- **Usage**: Replacing long, boring introductory paragraphs.
- **Style**: Large serif (or elegant sans), indented, asymmetrical placement.
- **Purpose**: To humanize the content and allow the user to skim the emotion of the page.

### 3. The Subheadline (The Guide)
- **Usage**: Introducing sections or framing paragraphs.
- **Style**: Medium size, bold, wide tracking (letter-spacing) if uppercase.
- **Purpose**: Clear wayfinding.

### 4. The Body Text (The Whisper)
- **Usage**: For the actual story details.
- **Style**: Smaller than typical web defaults (e.g., 14px-16px). Generous line-height (1.6 - 1.8). Narrow max-width (ch-60 to ch-65) for perfect reading comfort.
- **Purpose**: Deep reading for those who choose to engage.

### 5. The Statistic (The Anchor)
- **Usage**: Data points.
- **Style**: Massive numerals, incredibly tight tracking, accompanied by tiny, uppercase labels.
- **Purpose**: Immediate factual impact.

### 6. The Caption (The Detail)
- **Usage**: Accompanying every image.
- **Style**: Tiny (12px), often italicized or muted color.
- **Purpose**: To provide context without competing with the body text.

## Execution Notes
We will heavily rely on the contrast between **Massive Display Typography** and **Tiny Detail Typography**. We will strip away the "middle ground" font sizes that make the current site feel muddy.
