# Visual Film Language: Cinematography

The camera is the narrator. The framing dictates the scale of the story. We alternate framing constantly to force a change in the viewer's psychological distance. We never repeat the same framing consecutively.

## The Shot Sequence (Opening to Mid-Point)

### 1. Arrival: The Wide Shot (Establishing)
- **What Appears:** The entire viewport fills with an image of the river at sunrise. Mist covers the water. The trees on the far bank are silhouetted against the morning sky.
- **Duration:** 100vh (full screen).
- **Function:** Grounds the viewer in physical space.

### 2. The Hook: Extreme Close Up (Macro)
- **What Appears:** A photograph occupying only 20% of the screen width, positioned in the bottom right quadrant. The image shows the deep wrinkles of an elder's hands holding dried rice.
- **Duration:** 40vh.
- **Function:** Forces the viewer to look at texture and humanity.

### 3. The Context: Medium Shot
- **What Appears:** A 50% width image on the left. Two villagers stand waist-deep in the river, pulling a net. The background is slightly out of focus.
- **Duration:** 60vh.
- **Function:** Introduces action and daily life.

### 4. The Contrast: Landscape (Horizontal Dominance)
- **What Appears:** An image spans 100% of the screen width, but only 30% of the screen height (a thin horizontal slice). It shows a wide, flat rice field under an overcast sky.
- **Duration:** 40vh.
- **Function:** Slices the vertical scrolling motion with a harsh horizontal line.

### 5. The Anchor: Portrait
- **What Appears:** A 3:4 aspect ratio image, center-aligned, occupying 60% of the screen height. A direct gaze from a village leader into the lens. The lighting is warm side-lighting.
- **Duration:** 80vh.
- **Function:** Confrontation. The viewer stops scrolling to meet the eyes in the photograph.

## Rule of Progression
If the current shot is Wide, the next shot must be Close. If the current shot is Human, the next shot must be Nature. If the current shot is High Contrast, the next shot must be Low Contrast (Mist/Sky).
