# The Emotional Constitution

This document is the highest law of our interactive documentary. Every creative choice, every word written, every image chosen, and every pause created must obey these immutable laws of emotion.

## 1. Curiosity before Information
The visitor must always wonder "why" before we tell them "what." If we provide a statistic or a fact before the visitor has asked the question in their mind, we have failed. We build the question through imagery and tone, and only then do we provide the answer.

## 2. Emotion before Statistics
A number without a human face is just data. We must make the visitor feel the weight of the community, the depth of the history, and the reality of the landscape *before* we ever show them a population count or an agricultural yield. The heart opens the door for the mind.

## 3. Show before Explain
If an image can make the visitor feel the morning mist, do not write a paragraph describing the morning mist. If a portrait can convey the wisdom of an elder, do not write a biography explaining their wisdom. Use text only to elevate what the imagery has already established.

## 4. Every Scroll Must Earn the Next Scroll
The visitor owes us nothing. Their default state is to leave. Every downward movement of their thumb must be rewarded with a revelation. We do not scroll to move down a page; we scroll to turn the page of a story. If a movement does not provide a reward, it is a wasted movement.

## 5. One Unforgettable Moment Every 20–30 Seconds
The rhythm of a documentary relies on peaks and valleys. If the journey remains flat for too long, the visitor loses interest. At least twice a minute, the visitor must encounter something that makes them stop entirely: a breathtaking landscape, a profound human truth in a massive quote, or a sudden, profound silence.

## 6. People Scan Before They Read
No one arrives ready to read a textbook. They arrive ready to scan a magazine. We must design for the scanner. The massive typography, the pull quotes, and the imagery must tell a complete, emotional story on their own. The body paragraphs are a reward for those who choose to linger, not a requirement for understanding.

## 7. The Visitor Should Feel Something Before Learning Something
If a visitor leaves our documentary knowing the exact location of the village but feeling nothing, we have failed. If they leave not remembering the exact geography, but feeling a profound sense of peace and a longing to visit, we have succeeded. Emotion is the only metric that matters.
